Mortgage protection insurance vs. PMI: what Ohio homeowners should know

By Matt Beiling, licensed insurance agent. Updated October 23, 2026.

Short answer: PMI protects your lender if the loan isn't paid. It pays your family nothing if you die or get seriously ill. Mortgage protection insurance is coverage you own that pays your family, so they can keep the house.

If you bought your home with less than 20% down, there's a good chance you pay PMI every month. A lot of Ohio homeowners assume that means the house is "covered" if something happens to them. It isn't, and it's one of the most common misunderstandings I hear on calls.

What PMI actually does

Private mortgage insurance is required on most conventional loans when the down payment is under 20%. You pay the premium, but the lender is the one protected. If the loan goes into default, PMI helps the lender recover its loss.

FHA loans have something similar called mortgage insurance premium (MIP). Same idea: it protects the lender, not you.

What mortgage protection insurance does

Mortgage protection insurance is usually a term life insurance policy sized to your mortgage. If you die during the term, it pays your beneficiaries, typically your spouse or kids, and they decide how to use it. Most families use it to pay off or keep paying the house.

Many plans today also include living benefits. If you're diagnosed with a qualifying critical, chronic or terminal illness, the policy can pay part of the coverage while you're alive, so the mortgage and bills keep getting paid while you recover. What qualifies and how much pays out depends on the carrier and plan.

A note on the "mortgage life insurance" your lender offers

Some lenders and banks offer their own mortgage life insurance. Read it closely. With some of these policies the benefit goes straight to the lender, and the payout shrinks as your balance goes down while the premium stays the same. A policy you own through an agent usually pays your family directly and keeps a level benefit, which gives them more choices.

How to tell what you have

  • Look at your monthly mortgage statement. A line labeled PMI or MIP is lender protection.
  • If you think you have life coverage on the house, find the policy and check who the beneficiary is. If it's the lender, your family only gets the house paid down, not cash.
  • Coverage through work usually ends if you leave the job, so it may not last as long as your mortgage.

Who should look at mortgage protection

It's worth a look if your family couldn't keep up the house payment on one income, if you have a long time left on the loan, or if you'd want money coming in during a serious illness. Many plans don't require a medical exam, depending on your age and health.
